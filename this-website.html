Personal Portfolio Website

Role: UX Designer & Developer | Duration: 4 weeks | Tools: Figma, WordPress, HTML/CSS | Team: Solo (self-led)

1. Overview

In this project I designed and developed my personal portfolio website to showcase my work, story, passions and experience — marrying my UX design and web development skills into a clean, accessible, and responsive web presence.

2. Context / Problem

As a junior at the University of Michigan studying Urban Technology and User Experience Design, I needed a website that could act as a strong personal brand and portfolio asset for internships, leadership roles, and community-engagement opportunities. My previous online presence was fragmented, lacked a unified design system, and did not clearly reflect my interdisciplinary blend of UX, front-end development, accessibility, and urban systems interest.

The challenge was to build a site that communicated who I am, what I do, and why I do it, in a way that is both visually appealing and performant — while reflecting my values of accessibility, community impact, and thoughtful design.

3. Research & Insights

I conducted a competitive review of 10 peer designer portfolios (especially those emerging from UX/design programs) to understand common patterns: key pages (Portfolio, Passions, Experience, Contact), hero statements, project thumbnails, and narrative structure.

I surveyed peers and mentors for feedback: what they looked for in a portfolio site (clear role statement, easy navigation, strong visuals, responsive design, meaningful case studies).

  • A strong hero section immediately defines who I am and what I bring (e.g., “UX + Urban Tech student”). My site starts with “I am a junior studying Urban Technology and User Experience Design at the University of Michigan.”
  • Users expected clear navigation and simple site structure — my site uses four top-level pages (Portfolio · Passions · Experience · Contact).
  • Visual storytelling (photography, passion-sections, typography) helps create personal connection — my “Passions” page features architecture, photography, running, jazz, interior design, tennis.

An “aha” moment: combining my UX/dev skillset with my personal passions on one site allowed me to tell a more holistic story — not just “here are projects,” but “here is who I am and how I design and live.”

4. Ideation

I sketched multiple layout options: hero region with large statement → split intro & image; navigation options (hamburger vs full header); project grid vs timeline; passion segments with large imagery vs icon-based listing.

I created mind maps to map “what the user (employer/internship recruiter) wants to know” vs “what I want to say about myself.” Key axes: Role & Skills, Work (Portfolio), Story/Passions, Experience.

Decision: adopt a minimalist, clean aesthetic with sufficient white-space, clear typography, and intuitive navigation — to reflect my front-end cleanliness and UX sensibility.

Chose to structure the site as: Hero intro → “Be Happlimatic” mission statement → key pages (Portfolio / Passions / Experience / Contact). The mission statement (“Happlimatic”) gives a unique voice and narrative.

5. Design & Prototyping

Low-fidelity: Wireframed homepage structure in Figma (hero, mission statement, nav bar, footer).

Mid-fidelity: Created key pages in Figma – Portfolio grid, Passions page with sections, Experience page with timeline/history.

High-fidelity: Applied visual style — typography (clean sans-serif), color palette (neutral base + accent), imagery (passion-related illustrations/photos), responsive layouts for mobile/tablet/desktop.

Design Rationale

  • Accessibility: Ensured contrast ratios meet WCAG AA; navigation is keyboard-accessible; alt text for images; semantic HTML markup.
  • Layout: Simple header + hero image/text to instantly orient user.
  • Color/typography: Minimal color accent to keep attention on content; clear sans-serif for readability.
  • Storytelling: Personal mission statement (“Be Happlimatic”) to differentiate and express value.
  • Tools & implementation: Developed in WordPress + custom theme, integrated Figma designs, optimized images, ensured site performance and responsiveness.

6. Testing & Iteration

I invited 5 peers (other UX students) and 2 mentors (faculty or industry contacts) to review the site on desktop and mobile. They were asked to find key information (e.g., “What is Ty’s role? Where can I find his projects? How do I contact him?”).

  • All participants found the hero statement and nav clear.
  • Some found the mission statement (“Happlimatic”) intriguing but unclear — suggestion: brief tooltip or hover state to explain new word.
  • A few mobile users noted that the navigation collapsed into a hamburger icon but lacked visual indication of active page.

Iterations:

  • Added a micro-tooltip / hover-info for the “Happlimatic” word to clarify meaning.
  • Improved mobile nav: showing active page highlight, improved tap area for hamburger.
  • Optimized image loading (lazy load) and reduced page load time by ~30% (via compressed assets).

After iteration: Repeat review showed improved mobile usability, clearer narrative flow, better performance.

7. Outcome / Impact

The final site is a polished, responsive personal portfolio that clearly communicates my skills as a UX designer & front-end developer, and my interests in urban technology and community-oriented design.

The site has helped generate interest: I have shared the link in internship applications and networking opportunities, and the unified brand has enhanced my professional presence.

Beyond immediate goals, the project strengthened my ability to design and develop a full web experience from concept to deployment — a valuable demonstration of my dual competency in UX + dev.

It aligns with my goals of creating accessible, user-centered design with community and urban impact in mind.

8. Reflection

This project taught me the power of personal narrative in a portfolio — combining professional credentials with personal passions makes a stronger impression.

If I were to revisit it, I would include more in-depth case studies for each project (e.g., process, outcomes, metrics) and possibly integrate interactive elements (such as micro-animations) to better reflect my front-end skills.

I’m proud of how I translated my multidisciplinary background into a coherent digital presence and how the site reflects not just “what I do,” but “who I am.”

Moving forward, I’ll keep iterating the site as I gain new experience, ensuring the content and design continue to evolve with me.
